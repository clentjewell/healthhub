Functional fitness isn't about how a movement looks — it's about whether it actually helps you in everyday life. Here's why that distinction matters.

I guide people through full-body, functional workouts, and the question I get most is simple: what actually makes this "functional", as opposed to just exercise?

## What functional fitness actually is

It trains the body to move the way it needs to in daily life — bending, reaching, carrying, balancing, rotating — rather than isolating individual muscles. My classes blend strength, mobility, stability and coordination, with a strong focus on form, technique, and often-overlooked areas like core strength and pelvic floor awareness.

## What the evidence says

This is one area where the broader research is genuinely solid. A large Cochrane review found that exercise programs incorporating balance and functional or strength training meaningfully reduce fall rates in older, community-dwelling adults.<sup><a href="#reference">1</a></sup> I want to be precise here: that's evidence for this type of exercise generally, not a claim that my specific classes have themselves been the subject of a trial — but it's genuinely strong support for why this kind of training matters as people move through different life stages.

## What a class actually involves

Classes run in a small, supportive group, with clear cueing so you can build confidence in your movement at your own pace. I teach correct form first — intensity is guided by what's appropriate for you, not a fixed standard everyone's expected to meet from day one.

## What to expect as a complete beginner

You don't need any fitness background to start. Wear comfortable clothing you can move in, and let me know about any injuries or concerns before your first class so I can adapt accordingly.

## When it's worth checking with your GP first

If you have an existing injury, a recent surgery, or a specific health condition, it's worth checking with your GP before starting, so I can tailor the class appropriately for you.

## A place to start

If you're curious about functional fitness, come along to a class — everyone starts somewhere, and mine is a genuinely supportive place to do it.

## Reference

1. Sherrington C, Fairhall NJ, Wallbank GK, et al. Exercise for preventing falls in older people living in the community. *Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews*, 2019; Issue 1: CD012424.
