Eight practitioners, seven different disciplines, one address — if you're new to Health Hub Tweed Coast, it's reasonable to wonder where to actually start. Here's a simple way to think it through, based on what you're looking for rather than which service sounds most familiar.

## Start with what you're actually looking for

Most people fall into one of a few broad categories: a specific, ongoing health concern; a desire for more movement or physical strength; a need for calm and stress relief; or support for a family member. None of these are mutually exclusive, and many people end up combining services over time.

## If you have a specific, ongoing health concern

**[Dr Malcolm McKay](/our-practitioner/dr-malcolm-mckay/)** (Doctor of Acupuncture) is the practitioner to start with if you're managing an ongoing physical concern — joint or muscular pain, digestive issues, or general female or male health concerns — and want the support of an AHPRA-registered practitioner working within Traditional Chinese Medicine.

**[Amanda Ross](/our-practitioner/amanda-ross/)** (Naturopath) is a strong starting point for family health, children's health, and support through life stages such as perimenopause, working alongside your GP rather than replacing that care.

## If you want more movement and physical strength

**[Monique Carole](/our-practitioner/monique-carole/)** (Pilates & Body Balance) and **[Katie Verkerk](/our-practitioner/katie-verkerk/)** (Function & Flow, functional fitness) both focus on structured, purposeful movement — Monique with an emphasis on control and body awareness, Katie with an emphasis on full-body, everyday functional strength.

## If you want calm, stillness or stress relief

**[Alicia Grace](/our-practitioner/alicia-grace/)** (Yoga & Meditation, taught with a trauma-informed lens) and **[Kate Doedee](/our-practitioner/kate-doedee/)** (Hatha & Yin Yoga) both teach yoga styles suited to slowing down and supporting general wellbeing, while **[Pearl Blinco-Doffiny](/our-practitioner/pearl-blinco-doffiny/)** (Crystal Sound Bowls) offers a completely passive, relaxation-focused sound healing experience if movement isn't what you're after.

## If you're looking for something a little different

**[Rebecca Lindsay](/our-practitioner/rebecca-lindsay/)** (Embodied Movement & Dance) suits people wanting a more intuitive, feeling-led approach to movement, rather than a fitness or performance-based class.

## You don't have to choose just one

Because every practitioner runs their own service, there's no reason to limit yourself to a single discipline. Many Health Hub clients combine services over time — for example, acupuncture alongside a movement class or yoga — building a plan that suits their own goals rather than fitting into a single category.

## FAQs

**What if I'm not sure which practitioner is right for me?** Call the Hub directly on 0415 919 521, and we can point you toward the practitioner best suited to what you're looking for.

**Do I need to book with every practitioner to get the full benefit of a holistic approach?** No. Start with whichever service matches your immediate need, and add others later if it's useful.

**Can I combine services, like acupuncture and yoga?** Yes — many clients do, and it's one of the main advantages of a multi-disciplinary centre.
