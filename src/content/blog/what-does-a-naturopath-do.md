If you've never seen a naturopath, this is a fair question: what actually happens in a consultation, and how is it different from a GP visit?

Naturopathy gets described a lot of different ways, so let me explain what a naturopath actually does, day to day, at Health Hub Tweed Coast.

People often come to see me when they haven't seen results from visiting a doctor, or after they've been told their results are "normal" but they still don't feel right. That gap — between test results and how someone actually feels day to day — is a lot of where my work sits. Visiting a naturopath is not just for when someone would prefer a more "natural" approach.

## What naturopathy actually involves

My focus is family health across the whole lifespan — children through to older adults. I have particular interests in children's health, women moving through perimenopause and menopause, neurodivergent family health, right through to dementia and Alzheimer's disease.

Rather than treating a symptom in isolation, I look at sleep, stress, nutrition, lifestyle and medical history together, looking into the whole person and every different body system, because in my experience these areas rarely sit apart from each other.

## Naturopath or GP — how are these different?

They're not interchangeable, and I don't position naturopathy as a replacement for your GP. A GP diagnoses and medically treats illness. My role sits alongside that — looking at the everyday, ongoing side of health that a ten-minute GP appointment often doesn't have time for.

Most of the families I work with use both: a GP for anything requiring diagnosis or medical treatment, and naturopathic support for the rest.

## What the evidence says

I want to be straightforward here rather than vague. Naturopathy isn't one single intervention — I work on the body as a whole. Medical research often singles out just one intervention at a time, so it may not be representative of the results seen with a whole-body treatment approach. Some of what I do — nutrition and lifestyle guidance — aligns closely with mainstream public health guidance and has a solid evidence base behind it. Other traditional naturopathic approaches rest more on long-standing clinical experience — years, sometimes centuries, of observed patient results — than on laboratory study.

## What happens at a first consultation

Expect a proper conversation — your full medical history, your current medical concerns and what you wish to achieve — not a rushed, symptom-only chat. This takes time. Diving deeper allows me to see a bigger picture, often finding things that could be contributing to, or causing, your symptoms.

I offer both comprehensive and shorter acute consultations, online or in person, because not everyone needs the same starting point.

## When it's worth seeing your GP first

Naturopathy isn't the right first stop for everything. If something feels urgent, if symptoms are severe or rapidly changing, or if you or your child need a diagnosis, please see your GP first — I'm always glad to work alongside that care once anything urgent has been addressed. Naturopathy also works well alongside specialists for long-term chronic conditions.
